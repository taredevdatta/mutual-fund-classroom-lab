1. Start with the essentials

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it according to a stated investment objective.

Core concept

NAV

Net Asset Value is the per-unit value of a mutual fund scheme's net assets. It is not, by itself, a measure of whether a fund is cheap or expensive.

Investment method

SIP

A Systematic Investment Plan invests a fixed amount at regular intervals. It is a method of investing, not a separate type of mutual fund.

Cost

Expense ratio

The annual recurring cost charged by a scheme, expressed as a percentage of assets. Costs reduce the investor's net return.

Risk

Volatility

The extent to which an investment's value fluctuates. Higher expected return is not guaranteed and may involve higher risk.

Portfolio

Diversification

Spreading money across different assets or categories can reduce concentration risk, but cannot eliminate investment risk.

Investor behaviour

Time horizon

The period an investor expects to remain invested. Goals and time horizon should inform risk capacity and asset allocation.

Remember: Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. Read all scheme-related documents carefully. Past performance does not guarantee future returns.

2. Compare three fund categories

These are simplified teaching descriptions, not live schemes or product recommendations.

Growth-oriented

Equity funds

Primarily invest in shares. Values can fluctuate significantly. Usually discussed for longer investment horizons, depending on the investor's risk profile.

Debt-oriented

Debt funds

Invest mainly in fixed-income instruments. They carry risks including interest-rate and credit risk; they are not risk-free or guaranteed.

Mixed allocation

Hybrid funds

Combine equity and debt in varying proportions. Risk depends on the scheme's actual asset allocation and strategy.

5. Teacher guide

A ready-to-use 60–90 minute classroom sequence.

TimeTeacher actionStudent evidence of learning
10 minAsk: “Is a mutual fund the same as a bank deposit?” Elicit prior knowledge.Students distinguish market-linked investments from deposits.
15 minExplain NAV, SIP, expense ratio, diversification and risk.Students explain key terms in their own words.
20 minIntroduce Aarav's fictional profile; students allocate the ₹1,00,000.Students justify asset allocation using goal and risk comfort.
15 minChange time horizon and assumptions; discuss why outcomes change.Students interpret scenarios and identify model limitations.
10 minRun the quiz and discuss misconceptions.Quiz responses and corrected explanations.
10–20 minAsk groups to present one portfolio decision and one risk they considered.Short group justification / exit ticket.

Learning outcomes

  • Define NAV, SIP and expense ratio.
  • Differentiate equity, debt and hybrid fund categories at an introductory level.
  • Construct and justify a hypothetical allocation that totals 100%.
  • Explain why assumed returns are not guarantees and identify key model limitations.

Discussion prompts

  1. Would a 25-year-old and a person retiring next year necessarily choose the same allocation? Why?
  2. Why is a higher NAV not automatically evidence that a fund is expensive?
  3. How might expense ratios affect long-term outcomes?
  4. What information would you need before making a real investment decision?
Assessment idea: Ask students to submit a 150-word rationale for their allocation, including goal, time horizon, risk comfort, diversification and one limitation of the simulation.
